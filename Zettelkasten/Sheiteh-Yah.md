# Sheiteh-Yah
Sheiteh-Yah was a great empire that came to dominate much of western [[Oskora]]. Under [[Ur-Aptera]], its greatest king, the state engulfed many of its neighbors, beginning with dynastic arrangements that preceded the emergence of the [[Sheiteh-Yah-Simit-Sil-Hak dual monarchy|Sheiteh-Yah/Simit-Sil-Hak dual monarchy]]. Ultimately the empire collapsed following the events of the [[First Y'Ghatan War]].

Following the [[Cataclysm]], a new state emerged that claimed the heritage of Sheiteh-Yah. Though that state considered itself to be the territorial and spiritual continuation of the state discussed here, this post-cataclysm polity is generally referred to as [[New Sheiteh-Yah]]

#### Government 
Ruled by a king. The line of [[Shîyat Armagdar Nazrasiiman]] was the last dynasty prior to the events of the [[First Y'Ghatan War]] and the dissolution of Sheiteh-Yah's empire.

#### Military
Sheiteh-Yah fielded primarily infantry, with a large bulk of the troops made up of levies. These infantry levies were not a professional force, as it was common for farmers and townspeople to be called up in times of conflict. The cavalry of Sheiteh-Yah functioned primarily as scouts, screens, and skirmishers for their main force. Professional armed forces were drawn primarily from cites, and served as guards during times of peace. A large percentage of these professional soldiers owed their allegiance first to powerful nobles, merchants and priests, who in turn provided them to the monarch in a proto-feudal system. 

The right to maintain a body of soldiers could be revoked by the monarch. In practice, such a revocation was a delicate matter, as popular figures could and did incite civil war to protect that right. The ascension of [[Shîyat Armagdar Nazrasiiman]] to kingship, and the establishment of the [[Armagdar Dynasty]], was a result of such a conflict. 

#### History
A timeline of the lives of historical figures [is here](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/16PGe_Oto4ncwvr7v91zWfccnZrKvvnGp98F3AY3Y6WQ/edit?gid=0#gid=0).
Frequent conflicts arose between Sheiteh-Yah and [[Simit-sil-hak]], with three major wars shaping their history. There was a long history of small conflicts and border friction, with occasional reshuffling of borders between the states. One major turning point is the [[Fourth war between Sheiteh-Yah and Simit-Sil-Hak]], fought between [[Shîyat Armagdar III]] and the king of [[Simit-sil-hak]]. The resolution of this war saw [[Belat-Sunat]], the presumptive heir, married to [[Tulâdang of Simit-Sil-Hak]], and event that ultimately led to [[Simit-sil-hak]] being joined with [[Sheiteh-Yah]] after the formation of the [[Sheiteh-Yah-Simit-Sil-Hak dual monarchy]].

#### Economy
The prosperity of Sheiteh-Yah was greatly enriched by the fertile farmland along the [[Mahil River]], a result of periodic flooding. The river trade routes positioned them as a central hub between [[Simit-sil-hak]], the [[Black Rabbit Steppe]] and [[Darice]], though their lack of seafaring capabilities limited their expansion somewhat.

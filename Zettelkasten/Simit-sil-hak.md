# Simit-sil-hak
[[Simit-sil-hak]] was positioned at the eastern edge of the [[Black Rabbit Steppe]]. Its culture, influenced by both the steppe nomads and settled peoples, developed a unique linguistic and cultural blend. Many words and linguistic features of modern steppe people can be traced back to the influence of [[Simit-sil-hak]]. See [[Jaliki]] and [[Askuzai]].

#### Language
The language of Simit-sil-hak belongs to a separate language group from their neighbors in [[Sheiteh-Yah]]. See [[Simit-sil-hak Language Group]]. At the time of the [[Fourth war between Sheiteh-Yah and Simit-Sil-Hak]], the primary language spoken in [[Simit-sil-hak]] was [[Gora'in]].

#### Government 
Ruled by a king. 

#### Military
Although the long history and close ties between the [[Sheiteh-Yah]] and [[Simit-sil-hak]] meant that their armed forces were very similar in terms of armament and tactics, the armies of Sheiteh-Yah featured a much smaller contingent of cavalry and charioteers. The cavalry and horse archers of Simit-sil-hak were a fearsome force, employing the hunting methods and superior equestrianism of steppe peoples to field a flexible, mobile force. Employing feints, hit-and-run attacks, raids and disruption of logistics, Simit-sil-hak was frequently able to defeat Sheiteh-Yah in open battle. With comparatively less infantry, Simit-sil-hak would struggle to hold ground, as their mounted troops were unaccustomed to garrison duty. 

#### History 
See [[Sheiteh-Yah]] for more details. 
[[Simit-sil-hak]] fielded a strong force of cavalry and charioteers, and their military tactics favored flexibility and maneuverability. The relative lack of infantry forces meant that while [[Simit-sil-hak]] was often able to beat [[Sheiteh-Yah]] in the field of battle, they lacked the garrison forces to effectively control the more centralized, urbanized territory of their neighbor.

Under the reign of [[Ur-Aptera]], Simit-sil-Hak was merged into [[Sheiteh-Yah]], forming the [[Sheiteh-Yah-Simit-Sil-Hak dual monarchy]].

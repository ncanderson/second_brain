# Allatu the Archer
Allatu "the Archer" of [[Ana-sin-Amid]] was a self-styled king of a small territory, little more than a city-state with control of some surrounding land and smaller communities. 
#### Description
Rather than being a leader of a large state with great armies, Allatu has come down in the annals of [[Sheiteh-Yah]] history more for his strength of personality and strong character than the number of soldiers.
#### Personality
He was known for being a friendly, jovial person, full of laughter, who loved fighting and showing off his archery skills. He was well-loved by the troops he led. He had a love of battle, and often accompanied [[Belat-Sunat]], fighting alongside her in her chariot.

Alatu loved entertaining the troops with feats of archery and often went ranging alone or with a small band of scouts. He functioned less like a traditional lord or king and more like a adventuring hero, driven by personal glory and adventure more than political ambition or material wealth. He treated [[Sheiteh-Yah]]’s expansion almost like a grand adventure or game, more focused on personal exploits than formal command structure.
#### History
The story of [[Y'Ghatan|Y'Ghatan's]] meeting with Allatu is hard to verify. While records exist for other leaders such as [[Ganeka]], [[Belat-Sunat]] and [[Ea-Nasir]] prior to their absorption by [[Sheiteh-Yah]], Allatu only appears in a few dynastic records. 

However, after [[Ana-sin-Amid]] was absorbed by [[Sheiteh-Yah]], Allatu was given a command position and became good friends with [[Belat-Sunat]]. 

#### Motivation

- Ideals
Given his nature, it's likely that Allatu was content to relinquish his leadership of [[Ana-sin-Amid]]. He seemed to prefer hunting, drinking, and partying over the responsibilities of kingship.

- Bonds
Whether the relationship between Allatu and [[Belat-Sunat]] went deeper than camaraderie is unknown, though some speculated about it. Such talk was never spoken publicly or within earshot of the king or queen. There were even whispers that [[Belat-Sunat|Belat-Sunat's]] second child ([[Hâwaman]]), officially attributed to [[Ur-Aptera]] and claimed as his son, might have actually been Allatu's. In many ways, though never formally designated, he acted as a bodyguard for [[Belat-Sunat]], as they were often seen together.

# Fourth war between Sheiteh-Yah and Simit-Sil-Hak
#### Participants
Fought between [[Sheiteh-Yah]] and [[Simit-sil-hak]]. [[Sheiteh-Yah]] fielded an army of mostly infantry, with mounted and chariot elements serving primarily as screens. [[Simit-sil-hak]], with its close cultural and economic ties to the steppe, was able to field a far larger component of cavalry.
#### Lead-up
[[Shîyat Armagdar III]], the king of [[Sheiteh-Yah]], focused on grand building projects but neglected the military. By expanding infrastructure and initiating the construction of grand temples, centers of learning, and palaces, he expanded the economic and civic life of the people of Sheiteh-Yah, but ultimately neglected the training and equipment of his armed forces. It's unclear if there was a single inflammatory event for this war, but [[Simit-sil-hak]]certainly was aware of the state of Armagdar's armies, and saw an opportunity to attack.

#### Description
Principle fighting of the war began in 1812 BTR. Early stages of the war saw the armies of Sheiteh-Yah suffer a series of defeats in the field. Their poor equipment and training saw the armies seriously outmatched by the fluid, mobile tactics of [[Simit-sil-hak]]. As a result of [[Shîyat Armagdar III|Armagdar's]] building projects, there were ample fortifications to fall back to, and while those strong points allowed Sheiteh-Yah to slow the advance of [[Simit-sil-hak]], retreating behind walls yielded the initiative to their foe. In the middle stages of the war, [[Simit-sil-hak]] swept through eastern and southern Sheiteh-Yah, keeping their enemies pinned down while they raided poorly defended settlements. These fortifications did allow Sheiteh-Yah to protect the larger population centers, so in the long term, the war was not as devastating as it might have been to the population.

The turning point in the war came when [[Shîyat Armagdar III]] granted the right to levy troops to [[Harbishiku of Qateh-Yah]]. Having accumulated great wealth, he quickly levied several thousand soldiers from mining towns, as well as hiring foreign mercenaries ([[Allatu the Archer]] may have served as part of this company, as there are some accounts that he and [[Belat-Sunat]] and were acquainted prior to the incorporation of [[Ana-sin-Amid]] into [[Sheiteh-Yah]]). While unable to fully turn the tide of the conflict, these troops ensured the safety of the city of Sheiteh-Yah, and allowed Armagdar to begin resisting the raids of the [[Simit-sil-hak]] cavalry. This turning point triggered the beginning of a diplomatic resolution, as prior to this, Simit-sil-hak  was nearly unopposed. 

#### Outcome
Peace was secured by the marriage of [[Belat-Sunat]] to [[Tulâdang of Simit-Sil-Hak]] in 1808 BTR. [[Belat-Sunat]] became the queen of [[Simit-sil-hak]]. Additionally, Simit-sil-hak gained some small territorial concessions. Given Tulâdang's minority, the negotiations were carried out by members of his court. These ministers clearly intended for any legal issue of Belat-Sunat to bring a strong claim to the throne of Sheiteh-Yah. Belat-Sunat's popularity in Sheiteh-Yah was known in Simit-sil-hak, so it was very reasonable for Simit-sil-hak to think that any future  children would be welcomed in Sheiteh-Yah, even if that child was backed by Simit-sil-hak armies. It is possible that the idea of the dual monarchy that eventually emerged began not with Belat-Sunat herself, but in discussions about these potential futures.

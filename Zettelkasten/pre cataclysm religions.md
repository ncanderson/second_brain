# pre cataclysm religions
[[Magic]] and divinity are very closely linked in [[Oskora]]. The power of magic, for those who can access it, flows from a number of Realms, or [[Hold|Holds]]. They each bear an aspect, and within each are a number of positions conforming to a loose structure. The holders of these positions, named below, are mortals who have ascended, and in doing so, gained the power of a [[Gods|god]]. 

Each of these holds has a physical reality, and they can be visited. Not all are safe for denizens of the material world, indeed, some would likely mean instant death. 

Those who are able to access the power of the holds directly can wield awesome magical power, but the gods also reward the faithful with a measure of that same power. Often called [[The Concordat]], a god needs to share its power, for a god that has no worshippers slips into obscurity, and if that deity holds a position in one of the holds, it is vulnerable to usurpation.

When [[The Ancient Ones]] created [[The Twisted Strands]] and placed a kernel of consciousness into each of them, they did so out of delight in the potential for what could emerge. The emergence of consciousness was not preordained, but the potential existed. Each of the elemental spheres had the possibility of developing consciousness, as did the tapestry woven together from all of them—what we refer to as the material plane. Over time, the dialogue between these forces created the gods. Prayer and worship became a dialogue between these forces: the divine molding itself to the shape of the prayers of worshipers, and the worshipers taking something of the divinity they worshiped into themselves.

As this process unfolded, the divinities that emerged took on more human, elven, or other familiar forms, often growing out of organized religions. These religions, in turn, are mortal structures aligned with power, wealth, and influence. The very existence of these structures brings order to the chaotic elemental forces that make up the essence of the threads. So, when we talk about [[Magor the Shovel]] posing as [[Mentu-em-Asim]] and stealing prayers from those who were sacrificed, or [[Y'Ghatan]], what we see is a consciousness—Maegor—taking the form of a human, though the force he represents is far more expansive and fluid than that.

Over thousands of years, many divinities shifted and changed to meet the needs of their worshipers, while simultaneously influencing those needs. It’s very much a two-way street. The dialogue between the worshipers and the worshiped gives the gods both form and power. So, by presenting himself as [[Mentu-em-Asim]], [[Magor the Shovel]] was responding to the outflowing intention and emotion from those people, becoming something of what [[Mentu-em-Asim]] had been, simply by positioning himself as such.

Obviously, [[Mentu-em-Asim]] was jealous of this, feeling that something was being stolen from him. However, those feelings of jealousy are not inherently tied to the nature of death itself, but rather are an outgrowth of human worship and co-creation with human consciousness. This dynamic is what makes these gods so interesting. When contrasted with, say, a more elemental fire divinity—more animalistic and elemental in nature—you can see the stark contrast between these two types of divine forces. See all [[First Y'Ghatan War]] for more context on the specifics of magor and mentu in this context.

The existence of gods stems from when the ancient ones created the Twisted Strands, weaving in the elemental forces they delighted in and used to build the structure of the universe. Each of these strands, filled with their own unique delight, had the potential to form what we now term as gods.

These gods aren’t necessarily sentient by default, but they have the potential to become so. The gods we know today are the result of these elemental forces coalescing within their respective realms or holds. These forces, through interaction and merging, can become known entities to mortals, leading to greater awareness and physicality. This, in turn, creates a direct causal feedback loop between the gods and their worshipers. Much like a thought in your mind is safe, but a secret, once shared, can lose its control, so too do gods become more vulnerable once they interact with mortals.

As time has passed, many powerful beings have replaced the original elemental forces that emerged from the raw fabric of the universe. Each realm or plane has its own mini pantheon, with deities emerging and acting as they see fit—good or bad. Human religions, which form around these forces, are typically polytheistic, each god representing the aspects of life a person is drawn to.

It was rare in the ancient world for monotheistic religions to emerge. Instead, people were more likely to be drawn to the various forces that shaped their lives, forming the foundation of their pantheon. As a person’s devotion to a particular deity grows, that god may manifest more or less to them personally, giving rise to a personal revelation. On the broader cultural scale, religions tend to be more structured and concrete.

For example, coastal communities often have a federation of sea deities due to their reliance on the sea, its storms, and the ability to travel safely across it. Similarly, warriors often pray to death deities. The emergence of gods, much like the emergence of consciousness in animals, is a process shaped by interaction with their environment. The more sentient a deity becomes, the more power they can wield over mortals. But with that power comes the risk of losing their position to another deity or powerful being.

# Belat-Sunat
**Description:** A powerful vampire in the service of [[Y'Ghatan]], serving [[Sheiteh-Yah]]. 

A young Belat-Sunat, leading a cavalry charge during the [[Fourth war between Sheiteh-Yah and Simit-Sil-Hak]].

**History:** 
Born: 1838 BTR

Born the first child of [[Shîyat Armagdar III]] and was the assumed queen. While little is know about her youth, she was raised in court and learned history, science, diplomacy, and war. She was also a skilled warrior, favorite archery, horseback riding and chariot combat. One story recounts a challenge she issued, promising riches to anyone that could beat her in a chariot race. Though many of the nobility and members of the military took up her challenge, she beat them all, proving her exceptional skill.

In 1810 BTR, she was married off at 28 to [[Tulâdang of Simit-Sil-Hak]] as a peace offering and hostage, following a brief [[Fourth war between Sheiteh-Yah and Simit-Sil-Hak|war]] between [[Simit-sil-hak]] and [[Sheiteh-Yah]]. Though it grieved her, Belat-Sunat did eventually go through with the political marriage. The degree to which she consented is unknown; no record exists regarding her feelings on the matter. 

Simit-sil-hak took a much different view of the role of women than Sheiteh-Yah, and Belat-Sunat felt great pressure to present the Simit-sil-hak feminine ideal. Despite these pressures, she forged a strong relationship with the military command structure of Simit-sil-hak. It seems that her training with the army was indulged as an eccentricity, but those relationships Belat-Sunat forged meant that when her husband died, she was able to formalize her role as queen regent without much resistance. 

It is a testament to her diplomatic acumen and great patience that the foreigner was able to gain the degree of power that she did, and by all accounts, no suspicion about the death of [[Tulâdang of Simit-Sil-Hak]] fell on her despite the involvement of Belat-sunat and Ur-Aptera. Her late-stage pregnancy may have done much to alleviate any potential conflict - Simit-sil-hak royalists recognized that she was carrying the future king. The situation may have been much different had Belat-Sunat borne a girl. 

She gave birth to [[Nabat]] in 1805 BRT, at the age of 33. Styling herself the Queen Regent, she was the defacto ruler of Simit-sil-hak until her marriage to [[Ur-Aptera]]. On the death of Tulâdang, the few nobles who resisted Belat-sunat were powerless to take direct action, but many recognized the son of Belat-Sunat as the rightful king of Simit-sil-hak. Nabat was kidnapped by a faction of the nobility, and his ultimate fate is unknown. There were some internal uprisings, but Belat-Sunat was able to put the uprisings down without any outside assistance.

With the creation of the [[Sheiteh-Yah-Simit-Sil-Hak dual monarchy|Sheiteh-Yah/Simit-Sil-Hak dual monarchy]], she and her brother anointed each other monarchs in their own right, and Belat-sunat simply dropped the style of regent.
